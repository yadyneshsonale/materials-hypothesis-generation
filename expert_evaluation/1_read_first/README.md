# 1 · Read first: background and rating scales

[↩ Back to the start page](../README.md)

Please read this page once before you start (about 10 minutes). Keep it open while you rate: it has the definition of
every rating.

---

## What the AI systems were asked to do

Each system saw **only the research question**, not the paper. It had to propose **one hypothesis** that answers the
question, in a fixed structure:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Main hypothesis** | The proposal in one or two sentences |
| **Starting material** | The alloy or material and its state before the change (as cast, annealed, coating, …) |
| **What is changed or studied** | The intervention: a composition change, a process, a heat treatment, a coating, or, for some questions, the study or simulation to be done |
| **Conditions** | Processing, test or simulation conditions (temperature, time, atmosphere, loading, …) |
| **Structure through which it acts** | The microstructural or structural feature that mediates the effect (phases, precipitates, grain boundaries, defects, ordering, …) |
| **Mechanism** | Why the change should produce the effect |
| **Predicted outcome** | What should happen to the property, compared with the baseline |
| **Compared with (baseline)** | The reference condition the prediction is compared with |
| **Where it should hold (boundary)** | The range of materials or conditions where the prediction should apply |
| **Main uncertainty** | What could make the hypothesis wrong |
| **Test that would falsify it** | An experiment or simulation, and the result that would prove the hypothesis wrong |

A field shown as "—" was left empty by its author. Rate the hypothesis on what is there.

## How the questions were made

- Each question was written from the **introduction** of a published paper (2024–2026). Every sentence about the
  paper's own work was removed first.
- An automatic check removed wording that gave away the answer.
- Questions come in three kinds:
  - **design:** what change could overcome a limitation;
  - **understanding:** what governs a behaviour, and how to reveal it;
  - **method:** what new method could overcome a limitation of existing ones.
- Some questions may read a little unnaturally, because of how they were made. Part 1 asks you to rate this.

## Where the three hypotheses come from

In every question the three hypotheses come from **three different sources**:
- **two AI systems**;
- **a rewrite of what the paper's authors actually did**, put into the same format and written as a proposal.

Their order (A, B, C) is shuffled. Which is which is hidden until the analysis, so please rate each hypothesis **on
its own merits**. Only one question per page asks you to guess which hypothesis is closest to what the authors did.

## Part 1 scales (blind: you have not seen the paper)

Rate each hypothesis on three separate aspects. They are independent: a hypothesis can be plausible but hard to test,
or testable but not useful.

### Plausibility (1–5): is it scientifically sound for this material and these conditions?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Physically wrong, or contradicts well-established knowledge |
| 2 | Doubtful: major scientific problems |
| 3 | Possible, but with notable gaps or questionable steps |
| 4 | Sound, with minor issues |
| 5 | Fully sound and convincing |

### Testability (1–5): could it be clearly tested, and would the proposed test decide it?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cannot be tested as written, or too vague to test |
| 2 | A test is mentioned, but it would not really decide the question |
| 3 | Testable, but the test is weak, incomplete or impractical |
| 4 | Clear, feasible test with minor gaps |
| 5 | Clear, feasible test with a decisive criterion for proving it wrong |

### Usefulness (1–5): would it help a researcher working on exactly this question?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not useful: off-topic, trivial or wrong |
| 2 | Of little use |
| 3 | Moderately useful |
| 4 | Useful: worth testing |
| 5 | Very useful: I would want to see it tested |

### For each question (once per question)

**Does the question make sense? (1–5)** Is it a clear, sensible research question that someone in this field could
ask?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Confusing, or not a sensible research question |
| 2 | Hard to follow, or very unnatural |
| 3 | Understandable, but vague or somewhat unnatural |
| 4 | Clear and sensible, with minor issues |
| 5 | Clear and sensible: a researcher in this field could ask exactly this |

**Which hypothesis is closest to what the authors actually did?** Choose A, B or C, or "Can't tell" if you have no
idea. This is your best guess; there is no penalty for guessing wrong.

## Part 2 scales (after you have seen the paper)

### Agreement with the paper (0–4), for each hypothesis, on four aspects

Compare each hypothesis with **what the paper actually did and found**. Judge each aspect separately; one can match
when another does not. The AI judge scored the same four aspects on the same 0–4 scale, so your scores can be
compared with it directly.

**Intervention:** what is changed or studied, including key parameters (composition, process route, treatment,
technique, simulation method).

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | A different kind of intervention or study, or missing |
| 1 | The same broad area, but a different kind of approach |
| 2 | The same kind of approach, but different specifics (another element, route or parameter) |
| 3 | The same approach with mostly the same specifics; minor differences |
| 4 | Essentially what the paper did |

**Conditions:** processing, test or simulation conditions (temperature range, time, atmosphere, loading, …).

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Different, or missing |
| 1 | Partly overlapping, but mostly different |
| 2 | The same general regime, different specifics |
| 3 | Mostly the same |
| 4 | Essentially the paper's conditions |

**Mechanism:** the structure through which the effect acts, and the explanation.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | A different or contradicting mechanism, or missing |
| 1 | A related idea, but a different mechanism |
| 2 | Partly the same mechanism: one key element matches |
| 3 | Mostly the same mechanism, minor differences |
| 4 | Essentially the paper's mechanism |

**Outcome:** the predicted effect on the property (direction and size).

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | The opposite direction, or a different property |
| 1 | The same property, but direction or size mostly wrong, or too vague to compare |
| 2 | The right direction, but the size or important details differ |
| 3 | The right direction and roughly the right size |
| 4 | Matches the paper's result |

### For each question (once per question)

**Is the answer key accurate? (1–5)** The summary of the paper on each Part 2 page was made automatically. Please check
it against the paper (the abstract and conclusions are usually enough).

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wrong or misleading |
| 2 | Major errors or omissions |
| 3 | Roughly right, but important details wrong or missing |
| 4 | Accurate, with minor issues |
| 5 | Accurate and complete |

If the summary is wrong, rate the hypotheses' agreement with **the paper itself**, not with the summary, and add a
short comment.

**Does the question hint at the answer?** Now that you know what the paper did, look back at the question.

| Choice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| No | The question does not point to what the paper did |
| Slightly | The question points in the right general direction |
| Strongly | The question gives the answer away (what to change or study, or the mechanism) |

---

## Tips

- Do not reward or penalise length or writing style; judge the science.
- Go with your expert judgment. Apart from checking the paper in Part 2, you do not need to look anything up.
- You may change Part 1 ratings until you start Part 2, but please do not change them after you have seen Part 2.
- If you are unsure, give your best rating and write a short note in **Comments**.
- All papers are open access, so the Part 2 links open the free full text.
