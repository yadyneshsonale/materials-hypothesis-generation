## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> Starting from a W–Cu composite containing W particles or interconnected W ligaments within a Cu matrix, what single intervention could overcome phase separation and interfacial void formation, thereby improving thermal conductivity relative to the untreated composite under heat-transfer and irradiation conditions relevant to joints between W-based plasma-facing materials and Cu-based cooling components? Formulate one hypothesis that states the intervention, the proposed mechanism, the predicted comparative outcome, the applicable processing and service boundaries, supporting prior evidence, and a decisive falsification experiment.

Continued interfacial Ti extraction leaves W, which has the strongest positive heat of mixing with Cu, trapped as a W-rich passivating interlayer that arrests further exchange and supports stable bonding. Aligned Cu channels provide conductive pathways; the increase in thermal conductivity with temperature is attributed to reduced diffuse interfacial mismatch and increasing lattice-mediated transport overcoming declining electronic transport. ## The three hypotheses (the same as in Part 1)

---

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** Introduction of a Ti interlayer between W and Cu can overcome phase separation and interfacial void formation, improving thermal conductivity.
- **Starting material:** W-Cu composite with W particles or interconnected W ligaments within a Cu matrix
- **What is changed or studied:** Introduction of a Ti interlayer
- **Conditions:** Heat-transfer and irradiation conditions relevant to joints between W-based plasma-facing materials and Cu-based cooling components
- **Structure through which it acts:** Ti interlayer
- **Mechanism:** Improved wettability and bonding between W and Cu due to the Ti interlayer, reducing interfacial voids and enhancing thermal conductivity
- **Predicted outcome:** Improved thermal conductivity relative to the untreated composite
- **Compared with (baseline):** Untreated W-Cu composite
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Processing conditions: temperature, pressure, and duration of Ti interlayer introduction; Service conditions: temperature, irradiation dose, and duration
- **Main uncertainty:** Optimal thickness and microstructure of the Ti interlayer, potential effects of irradiation on the Ti interlayer
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Comparison of thermal conductivity between W-Cu composites with and without a Ti interlayer, under relevant heat-transfer and irradiation conditions
  - *Measurements:* Thermal conductivity; Interfacial microstructure; Irradiation-induced defects
  - *Falsified if:* If the thermal conductivity of the W-Cu composite with a Ti interlayer is not significantly higher than that of the untreated composite, the hypothesis is falsified.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** Liquid-metal dealloying of a directionally segregated WTaVTi precursor in molten Cu will selectively replace Ti/V-rich channels with Cu while preserving refractory ligaments and forming a W-rich interfacial barrier. The resulting aligned, bicontinuous RHEA–Cu composite should suppress interfacial voiding, conduct heat better than the untreated precursor, exceed pure W above about 650 °C, and provide RHEA ligaments with greater He-blister resistance than W.
- **Starting material:** The precursor will be nominally equiatomic, single-BCC WTaVTi with directionally solidified W/Ta-rich dendrites occupying about 70 vol.% and connected Ti/V-rich interdendritic channels occupying about 30 vol.%.
- **What is changed or studied:** The precursor will undergo liquid-metal dealloying by immersion in molten Cu, using the Ti-rich interdendritic regions as preferential sacrificial pathways for Cu ingress and directional channel formation.
- **Conditions:** Dealloying will be conducted near 1200 °C in a sealed vacuum environment. Thermal performance will be considered from room temperature to about 800 °C, with behavior above 1000 °C treated only as extrapolative. Irradiation screening will use 25 keV He+ implantation to fluences near 8 × 10^17 atoms cm−2.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The intervention should produce an aligned bicontinuous architecture containing roughly 64 vol.% BCC WTaVTi-based RHEA ligaments and 36 vol.% nearly pure FCC Cu channels. An approximately 300 nm W-rich interlayer should form at RHEA/Cu interfaces, with minor Ti-rich HCP precipitates behind it.
- **Mechanism:** Ti should preferentially mix with Cu and dissolve from interdendritic regions, whereas comparatively Cu-immiscible W, Ta, and V should remain in continuous ligaments. Continued Ti extraction should trap W at the reaction front, creating a passivating interlayer that arrests exchange and stabilizes bonding. Aligned Cu channels should provide efficient heat paths, while reduced diffuse interfacial mismatch and increasing lattice transport should raise conductivity with temperature. Lattice distortion, compositional complexity, and high ligament strength should alter defect migration and recombination, reducing He-driven swelling and cracking.
- **Predicted outcome:** Thermal conductivity should rise from an untreated value near 20 W m−1 K−1 to about 75 W m−1 K−1 at room temperature, approximately 120 W m−1 K−1 near 650 °C, and 140 W m−1 K−1 near 800 °C. RHEA ligaments should show roughly 30% less swelling at blistering than W and delay blistering from about 8.0 to 8.5 × 10^17 atoms cm−2.
- **Compared with (baseline):** Comparators will include untreated WTaVTi, pure W, pure Cu, conventional W–Cu composites, and WTa and WTaV for irradiation comparisons.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis will apply to directionally segregated WTaVTi processed near 1200 °C. Irradiation claims will be limited to low-energy He+ screening of precursor dendrites as ligament proxies, not the finished composite, neutron exposure, or vacancy-driven void swelling. Composite strength will remain unclaimed.
- **Main uncertainty:** The W-rich layer may not remain uniformly passivating over millimeter-scale penetration, potentially causing incomplete replacement or residual interfacial defects.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Process matched precursor sections with and without molten-Cu dealloying, then compare architecture, conductivity, and He response against pure W and conventional W–Cu controls.
  - *Measurements:* SEM/TEM-EDS and XRD for Cu replacement, phase continuity, and the W-rich interface; Temperature-dependent thermal conductivity; AFM swelling and blister/crack onset after 25 keV He+ implantation
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis will be rejected if continuous Cu channels and a W-rich interface do not form, or if conductivity fails to exceed untreated WTaVTi substantially and pure W near 650–800 °C, or if RHEA dendrites do not reduce swelling or delay blistering relative to W.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** Pre-coating tungsten particles with a nanoscale titanium layer before composite consolidation will induce a graded W–Ti–Cu interfacial region that suppresses phase separation and interfacial void formation, thereby enhancing thermal conductivity under heat-transfer and irradiation conditions relevant to plasma-facing joints.
- **Starting material:** A W–Cu composite with distinct tungsten particles or interconnected tungsten ligaments dispersed in a copper matrix, which in the untreated state shows phase separation and interfacial voids under service conditions.
- **What is changed or studied:** Apply a uniform nanoscale (e.g., ~5 nm) titanium coating onto tungsten particles prior to powder consolidation and sintering.
- **Conditions:** Processing via powder metallurgy under an inert atmosphere with controlled sintering conditions; service conditions up to moderate temperatures (e.g., ~500°C) and exposure to irradiation levels typical of plasma-facing component environments.
- **Structure through which it acts:** A graded interface developing from the titanium layer that bonds with both tungsten and copper, stabilizing the interfacial microstructure and acting as a diffusion barrier.
- **Mechanism:** The reactive titanium coating improves the wettability between tungsten and copper; during consolidation, titanium forms a transitional interphase that reduces interfacial energy and limits fast diffusion paths. This minimizes void nucleation and phase separation under thermal and irradiation stress, resulting in an interface that supports higher thermal conductivity.
- **Predicted outcome:** Under identical heat-transfer and irradiation conditions, the titanium-coated composites are predicted to show fewer interfacial voids and improved phase integrity, which translates to enhanced thermal conductivity relative to untreated composites.
- **Compared with (baseline):** Untreated W–Cu composites that typically develop interfacial voids and phase separation under elevated temperature and irradiation conditions, leading to suboptimal thermal performance.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The expected benefits are likely confined to processing routes (e.g., powder metallurgy and controlled sintering) that maintain the integrity of the titanium coating and within service conditions up to ~500°C and the tested irradiation levels. The effectiveness outside these processing or service boundaries remains uncertain.
- **Main uncertainty:** There is uncertainty regarding the optimal titanium coating thickness, the long-term stability of the graded interface under extended irradiation, and how scaling up the coating process might affect the interfacial chemistry.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare two batches of W–Cu composites: one with tungsten particles pre-coated with a ~5 nm Ti layer and one without coating. Process both batches identically via powder metallurgy under an inert atmosphere and sinter under controlled conditions. Subject the resulting composites to a standardized heat-treatment cycle and irradiation regime simulating plasma-facing service conditions.
  - *Measurements:* Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to assess interfacial microstructure and detect voids.; Thermal conductivity measurements under operational temperatures.; Irradiation-induced defect density and phase stability analysis.
  - *Falsified if:* If the Ti-coated composites do not exhibit a statistically significant reduction in interfacial voids and phase separation or fail to achieve improved thermal conductivity relative to the untreated composites under identical testing conditions, then the hypothesis is falsified.
