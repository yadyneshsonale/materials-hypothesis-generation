## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> For multicomponent high-entropy alloys in solid and liquid states at and near their high melting temperatures, what single testable methodological intervention could overcome the inability of EAM references to represent the many atomic configurations of the ab initio liquid? Explain why it should improve reference–target phase-space overlap, predict the reduction in thermodynamic-integration path mismatch relative to TOR-TILD’s EAM references, define the applicable composition and temperature ranges, identify supporting evidence from elemental or binary calculations, and specify a decisive free-energy benchmark that would falsify the hypothesis.

## The three hypotheses (the same as in Part 1)

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** Using machine learning potentials trained on ab initio molecular dynamics simulations can improve the representation of atomic configurations in high-entropy alloys, especially in the liquid state near their high melting temperatures.
- **Starting material:** High-entropy alloys in solid and liquid states
- **What is changed or studied:** Machine learning potentials trained on ab initio molecular dynamics simulations
- **Conditions:** High melting temperatures, near and at the melting point
- **Structure through which it acts:** Improved representation of atomic configurations
- **Mechanism:** Enhanced reference-target phase-space overlap due to better representation of liquid structures
- **Predicted outcome:** Reduced thermodynamic-integration path mismatch relative to TOR-TILD's EAM references
- **Compared with (baseline):** TOR-TILD's EAM references
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Applicable composition and temperature ranges specific to high-entropy alloys
- **Main uncertainty:** Lack of direct evidence for high-entropy alloys; reliance on elemental or binary calculations for support
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Comparison of free-energy calculations using machine learning potentials and EAM references for a high-entropy alloy
  - *Measurements:* Free-energy differences; Phase-space overlap metrics
  - *Falsified if:* If the machine learning potentials do not show a significant reduction in thermodynamic-integration path mismatch compared to EAM references, the hypothesis is falsified.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** For disordered BCC TaVCrW and its liquid, replacing TOR-TILD’s EAM references with a high-fidelity moment tensor potential (MTP), and evaluating the MTP-to-DFT correction by free-energy perturbation (FEP), would provide sufficient reference–target phase-space overlap to determine melting free energies without slowly convergent EAM-to-DFT thermodynamic integration (TI).
- **Starting material:** The target material would be the nominally equiatomic refractory high-entropy alloy TaVCrW, considered as a chemically disordered BCC solid and as a multicomponent liquid near melting.
- **What is changed or studied:** An MTP would replace the empirical EAM liquid reference, and FEP would replace the expensive ab initio-MD TI correction. The MTP training objective would be an energy error below approximately 1 meV/atom relative to DFT configurations.
- **Conditions:** The free-energy framework would use DFT with both GGA-PBE and LDA at 0 GPa. Solid-state short-range order (SRO) would be assessed over approximately 2000–2400 K, spanning the relevant high-temperature solid and liquid regimes.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The mediator would be an MTP whose configurational energy distribution closely matches the ab initio distribution across the diverse local environments of multicomponent solid and liquid phases.
- **Mechanism:** Improved phase-space overlap would reduce the variance of MTP–DFT energy differences, allowing FEP to converge rapidly and avoiding the long, mismatched TI path required by EAM references. Electronic Gibbs-energy contributions associated with a density-of-states gap near the Fermi level, together with SRO stabilization of the solid, would shift the solid–liquid free-energy crossing.
- **Predicted outcome:** FEP and TI free energies should agree within about 0.5 meV/atom, while computational demand should fall by roughly 80%, potentially corresponding to an approximately 20-fold speedup for the relevant correction. Electronic excitations should shift melting by about 50–60 K, while SRO stabilization of roughly 2–3 meV/atom should produce an additional approximately 25 K shift. Predicted melting temperatures could remain about 160–190 K below extrapolated CALPHAD values while still lying within their estimated uncertainty.
- **Compared with (baseline):** The comparator would be original TOR-TILD using two EAM references and computationally expensive TI based on ab initio molecular dynamics.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Applicability would be restricted to Ta–V–Cr–W compositions and temperatures represented in the MTP training domain. Magnetic fluctuations would be assumed negligible at high temperature. Functional-dependent underbinding or overbinding rules may fail for V and Cr, limiting comparison with CALPHAD.
- **Main uncertainty:** The main uncertainty would be incomplete MTP coverage of rare liquid configurations, which could increase FEP variance and bias the melting free energy.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* For an elemental or binary validation system, particularly VW, the same DFT states would be evaluated independently by MTP-based FEP and EAM-based TI under matched high-temperature conditions.
  - *Measurements:* FEP–TI free-energy difference per atom; Variance of MTP–DFT energy differences; Electronic-excitation contribution to the melting-temperature shift
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis would be rejected if the benchmark FEP and TI free energies differed by more than approximately 0.5 meV/atom, or if poor MTP–DFT overlap prevented stable FEP convergence.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** Replacing the standard EAM reference with a machine‐learned interatomic potential (such as a neural network potential trained on a wide range of ab initio configurations) will improve phase–space overlap in thermodynamic integration for high-entropy alloys at high temperatures.
- **Starting material:** Multicomponent high-entropy alloys in both solid and liquid states near their melting temperature.
- **What is changed or studied:** Develop and employ a machine-learning (ML) interatomic potential trained on ab initio data that covers the diverse local atomic configurations found in the liquid state.
- **Conditions:** This approach is applicable to alloys with 5 or more principal elements (typical high-entropy alloys) and is focused on temperature ranges near the melting point (roughly 0.85–1.05× the melting temperature) where liquid configurations are most diverse.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The ML potential acts as an intermediary model, capturing many-body and local-environment effects that are missed by the EAM framework, thereby bridging the configurational gap between the ab initio target and the reference system.
- **Mechanism:** Because the ML potential is trained on a broad set of ab initio configurations, it better represents the energetic landscape and structural variability of the liquid state than traditional EAM potentials. This enhanced representation improves the overlap between sampled configurations of the reference and target systems, reducing the thermodynamic-integration path mismatch.
- **Predicted outcome:** A reduction in the mismatch of the thermodynamic integration path relative to TOR-TILD’s EAM references, expected to be on the order of 30–50%, leading to free energy estimates that more closely reproduce the target ab initio values.
- **Compared with (baseline):** TOR-TILD calculations that currently employ EAM reference potentials.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The method may be less effective if the ML potential is trained on a dataset that does not fully capture rare or extreme configurations, or in systems with additional complexities (e.g., significant electronic structure effects) that fall outside the range of the training data. Also, performance might vary outside high-temperature regimes.
- **Main uncertainty:** There is uncertainty in the precise quantitative reduction in TI path mismatch and in the transferability of the ML potential to all high-entropy alloy systems, due to potential data limitations and the risk of overfitting in complex multi-component systems.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Conduct a comparative thermodynamic integration study on a representative high-entropy alloy sample near its melting point using both the standard EAM reference (as in TOR-TILD) and the new ML potential. Compare the free energy profiles and evaluate the phase–space overlap between the reference and the ab initio target system.
  - *Measurements:* Free energy changes along the thermodynamic integration path; Metrics quantifying phase-space overlap (e.g., distribution similarity of key structural descriptors)
  - *Falsified if:* If the ML potential does not produce a statistically significant (≥30%) reduction in integration path mismatch or yields free energy values that do not converge more closely to the ab initio benchmark than those obtained with the EAM reference, the hypothesis is falsified.
