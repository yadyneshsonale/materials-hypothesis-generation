## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> For a multi-phase eutectic high-entropy alloy exposed to simultaneous wear and corrosion at elevated temperature, what single mechanistic hypothesis could explain the accelerated coupled degradation and why it occurs? Specify a controlled comparison capable of distinguishing the proposed explanation, predict the changes in total degradation and the individual wear and corrosion responses relative to a matched baseline, define the relevant boundary conditions, identify supporting evidence, and give a clear falsification criterion.

## The three hypotheses (the same as in Part 1)

---

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** Synergistic effects between wear and corrosion at elevated temperatures accelerate degradation in multi-phase eutectic high-entropy alloys due to the formation of micro-galvanic cells and mechanical disruption of the protective oxide layer.
- **Starting material:** Multi-phase eutectic high-entropy alloy with a polished surface
- **What is changed or studied:** Simultaneous wear and corrosion exposure at elevated temperature
- **Conditions:** Elevated temperature (500°C), wear rate of 10 mm/s, and corrosive environment (3.5% NaCl solution)
- **Structure through which it acts:** Formation of micro-galvanic cells and mechanical disruption of the protective oxide layer
- **Mechanism:** Synergistic effects between wear and corrosion
- **Predicted outcome:** Accelerated coupled degradation, with a 30% increase in total degradation, a 20% increase in wear rate, and a 40% increase in corrosion rate relative to the baseline
- **Compared with (baseline):** Single-phase wear or corrosion exposure at room temperature
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Temperature range: 400-600°C, wear rate range: 5-15 mm/s, corrosive environment: 1-5% NaCl solution
- **Main uncertainty:** Limited understanding of the specific micro-galvanic cell formation and mechanical disruption mechanisms
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Controlled comparison of simultaneous wear and corrosion exposure at elevated temperature versus single-phase wear or corrosion exposure at room temperature
  - *Measurements:* Total degradation; Wear rate; Corrosion rate
  - *Falsified if:* If the total degradation, wear rate, and corrosion rate do not increase by at least 20% under simultaneous wear and corrosion exposure at elevated temperature compared to the baseline, the hypothesis is falsified

---

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** Simultaneous wear removes the protective oxide films at the interphase (eutectic) boundaries of the high-entropy alloy, which then become active sites for accelerated galvanic corrosion. This locally enhanced corrosion degrades the interphase cohesion, further increasing the susceptibility to wear. Together, these feedback processes lead to accelerated overall degradation.
- **Starting material:** A multi-phase eutectic high-entropy alloy exhibiting thermally-stabilized, protective oxide films that form on its phase surfaces under ambient or mildly aggressive conditions.
- **What is changed or studied:** Expose identical alloy specimens to combined mechanical wear and a corrosive environment at elevated temperature versus exposure to the corrosive environment alone (with wear minimized or controlled to preserve the oxide film).
- **Conditions:** An elevated temperature environment (e.g., around 600°C) combined with a corrosive medium and controlled mechanical abrasion that is sufficiently aggressive to erode the oxide layers but within service limits.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The interphase boundaries of the eutectic structure, which serve as galvanic couples once their protective films are compromised.
- **Mechanism:** Wear-induced removal of the protective oxide films at interphase boundaries exposes reactive metal surfaces. This exposure creates local galvanic cells due to inherent potential differences between the phases. The resulting accelerated corrosion weakens the microstructural interfaces, increasing susceptibility to further wear, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop of coupled degradation.
- **Predicted outcome:** Relative to a baseline where corrosion proceeds without significant mechanical film removal, the coupled environment is predicted to show an increased overall degradation (i.e., higher weight loss), with more pronounced wear tracks and deeper localized corrosion pits at interphase regions.
- **Compared with (baseline):** An exposure condition where the alloy is subjected solely to the corrosive environment at the same temperature without applied mechanical wear (or with minimal wear that does not disrupt the protective oxide), serving as the reference state for coupled versus independent degradation processes.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The coupling effect is expected only when the applied wear rate exceeds the oxide film reformation rate at elevated temperature. Below this threshold, or if the corrosion environment is not sufficiently aggressive to establish a galvanic couple upon film removal, the accelerated degradation mechanism may not dominate.
- **Main uncertainty:** The precise quantitative contributions of wear versus corrosion and the critical wear rate necessary for disrupting the oxide layer remain uncertain. Additionally, the variation in interphase potentials within the multi-phase eutectic microstructure may yield variable effects across different material compositions.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare two sets of identical multi-phase eutectic high-entropy alloy specimens. Subject one set to simultaneous wear and corrosion (using a controlled abrasion system in a corrosive, high-temperature environment) and the other set to corrosion only (ensuring that the oxide film remains largely intact). Periodically characterize surface damage, measure weight loss, and assess corrosion pit depths, with special attention to the interphase regions.
  - *Measurements:* Weight loss over time; Microscopic (SEM) imaging of interphase boundaries; Corrosion pit depth and distribution analysis; Surface profilometry of wear tracks
  - *Falsified if:* If no statistically significant difference in the degree of interphase degradation (or overall weight loss and pit depth) is observed between the specimens subjected to simultaneous wear and corrosion and the corrosion-only specimens (when controlling for oxide film presence), then the hypothesis that wear-induced oxide removal is responsible for enhanced galvanic corrosion and accelerated degradation is falsified.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** Accelerated high-temperature tribo-corrosion will arise from repeated formation, fracture, and removal of discontinuous oxide/tribo-layer patches, rather than from corrosion or mechanical wear acting independently. AlCoCrFeNi2.1 should resist this coupling better than duplex stainless steel 2205 because its thermally stable lamellar microstructure should maintain homogeneous Cr availability and rapid passive-film repair.
- **Starting material:** Mirror-polished, as-cast AlCoCrFeNi2.1 containing about 70% L12 and 30% B2 in a fine lamellar structure, with 2–3 µm B2 lamellae and nanoscale Cr-rich precipitates, will be compared with hot-rolled DS2205 containing approximately equal γ-FCC austenite and δ-BCC ferrite.
- **What is changed or studied:** Both alloys will receive three rapid heating/quenching cycles to about 1100 °C, followed by immersed reciprocating tribo-corrosion against alumina. The controlled comparison will match thermal conditioning, temperature, load, frequency, counter-body, and aqueous environment.
- **Conditions:** Tests will use deionized water at 25, 50, and 100 °C, loads of 5–15 N, frequencies of 3–9 Hz, and a 3.5 mm stroke. The principal temperature comparison will use 5 N and 5 Hz, with post-sliding immersion to assess repassivation.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The HEA should retain near-initial L12/B2 fractions, whereas DS2205 could undergo substantial γ-to-δ redistribution. The interfacial mediator should shift from abrasive contact at 25 °C, to a stable passive film or compacted low-shear tribo-layer near 50 °C, and to discontinuous, mechanically unstable oxide patches at 100 °C.
- **Mechanism:** At 25 °C, relatively slow film repair should permit two-body abrasion. Near 50 °C, thermally accelerated repassivation without extensive brittle-film disruption should promote polishing wear and minimum loss. At 100 °C, repeated film growth, rupture, and entrainment as debris should produce oxidative or third-body wear, friction instability, and coupled degradation.
- **Predicted outcome:** The HEA should undergo only a few percent phase redistribution, versus roughly 35 percentage points of γ-to-δ change in DS2205. Its wear rate should be about one order of magnitude lower, with lower friction: approximately 0.2, 0.18, and 0.25 at 25, 50, and 100 °C, compared with roughly 0.4, 0.32, and 0.4 for DS2205. For both alloys, total wear-volume loss should follow 50 °C &lt; 25 °C &lt; 100 °C. The HEA should also show nobler, steadier open-circuit potential, faster repassivation, and higher polarization resistance.
- **Compared with (baseline):** DS2205 under identical conditioning and sliding conditions will be the primary baseline; 25 °C and the as-cast phase fractions will provide within-alloy environmental and structural references.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis will apply to transient thermal-shock conditioning and reciprocating contact in low-ionic-strength water over 25–100 °C, 5–15 N, and 3–9 Hz. It will not establish equilibrium or continuous-service stability at 1100 °C, oxidation kinetics, or behavior in chloride-, sulfate-, or other ion-containing industrial waters.
- **Main uncertainty:** The extent to which the 50 °C minimum depends on tribo-layer compaction rather than passive-film electrochemistry may remain uncertain.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Conduct matched tribo-corrosion tests on both thermally shocked alloys at 25, 50, and 100 °C under 5 N and 5 Hz.
  - *Measurements:* Interferometric wear volume and wear rate; Coefficient of friction; Open-circuit potential and post-sliding repassivation; Polarization resistance; Wear-track morphology and debris mode; Pre- and post-shock phase fractions
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis will be falsified if 100 °C does not increase wear relative to 50 °C together with unstable film rupture or third-body morphology, or if the HEA lacks both substantially lower wear and more stable electrochemical recovery than matched DS2205.
