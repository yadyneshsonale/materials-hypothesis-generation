## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> For additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 high-entropy alloy with an fcc matrix, what testable mechanistic hypothesis could explain what governs degradation during elevated-temperature exposure to oxidizing or reducing gases and why catastrophic failure may occur? Specify a controlled change that would reveal the governing behavior, predicted observations relative to the starting-state baseline, applicable exposure boundaries, supporting prior evidence, and a clear falsification criterion.

## The three hypotheses (the same as in Part 1)

---

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** In additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3, elevated-temperature exposure in oxidizing versus reducing gas environments alters the formation and stability of a protective surface oxide scale. A robust, continuous Al/Cr-rich oxide that forms under moderately oxidizing conditions slows degradation, while reducing or excessively oxidative conditions disrupt its formation or adherence. The resulting compromised scale leads to internal oxidation, stress concentration, and ultimately catastrophic failure via rapid crack propagation.
- **Starting material:** As-produced, fcc-matrix high-entropy alloy with a fairly homogeneous elemental distribution and residual stresses from the additive manufacturing process.
- **What is changed or studied:** Expose specimens to carefully controlled atmospheres where oxygen partial pressure is systematically varied (e.g., switching from a moderately oxidizing gas to a reducing gas or vice versa) at elevated temperatures.
- **Conditions:** Elevated temperatures (e.g., 700–900 °C) with controlled gas environments ranging from mildly oxidizing (e.g., air or diluted O2) to reducing (e.g., H2-containing atmosphere) conditions.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The integrity and composition of the surface oxide layer (specifically, formation and adhesion of an AlCr-rich oxide scale) that mediates the alloy’s resistance to further degradation.
- **Mechanism:** Under moderately oxidizing conditions, a coherent, adherent AlCr oxide scale develops that protects the underlying fcc matrix. Under reducing or excessively oxidative conditions, this scale becomes discontinuous or poorly adherent, permitting internal oxidation and element depletion, which increases intergranular stresses and facilitates crack initiation and propagation.
- **Predicted outcome:** Specimens exposed to the optimal oxidizing conditions will develop a protective oxide scale and show slower degradation (as measured by weight gain/loss, microstructural integrity, and mechanical property retention), whereas specimens in reducing or overly oxidative conditions will show evidence of compromised oxide layers, accelerated internal oxidation, embrittlement, and ultimately catastrophic failure (e.g., rapid drop in mechanical strength and crack formation).
- **Compared with (baseline):** The as-manufactured alloy exhibits a homogeneous microstructure with residual stresses but without significant oxide scale formation or catastrophic failure in the absence of extreme gas environments.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The predicted behavior is expected at temperatures above ~700 °C and is sensitive to gas-phase oxygen activity; deviations outside these exposure boundaries (e.g., significantly lower temperatures or near-inert atmospheres) may not trigger the same degradation mechanisms.
- **Main uncertainty:** The exact temperature thresholds and the quantitative influence of slight variations in oxygen partial pressure on oxide scale formation and failure kinetics remain uncertain without targeted empirical calibration.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare a series of additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 samples and expose them to controlled furnaces set at 700–900 °C under varying gas compositions: one set in a moderately oxidizing atmosphere (e.g., air or diluted O2) and another set in a reducing atmosphere (e.g., H2-containing gas). Monitor the evolution of oxide scale formation, mechanical properties, and crack propagation over time.
  - *Measurements:* Surface oxide morphology and composition via SEM/EDS and XRD analysis.; Weight gain/loss measurements to infer oxidation rates.; Mechanical property assessments (e.g., tensile strength, fracture toughness) before and after exposure.; In situ or post-mortem analysis of crack initiation and propagation using microscopy.
  - *Falsified if:* If the evolution of the surface oxide scale, internal oxidation patterns, and the associated mechanical degradation do not differ as predicted between the oxidizing and reducing conditions, or if no correlation between scale integrity and catastrophic failure is observed, then the hypothesis is falsified.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** Precipitation-induced Cr heterogeneity will govern degradation of additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 during elevated-temperature oxidation. Aging will create Cr-depleted precipitates and interfaces that delay establishment of a continuous Cr2O3 barrier, increasing oxygen ingress and mass gain; above about 900°C, defect-assisted penetration of that barrier could promote extensive internal oxidation and potentially catastrophic scale failure.
- **Starting material:** The starting alloy will be selective-laser-melted Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 from prealloyed powder, with greater than 97% density and a single-fcc cellular microstructure containing dislocation-rich cell walls.
- **What is changed or studied:** The controlled change will be direct aging near 750°C. This treatment should generate roughly 25–30 vol% dual nanoprecipitates: homogeneous Ni/Ti-enriched L12 particles and larger Al/Ti-enriched, Cr/Fe-depleted L21 particles concentrated near cell walls. Oxidation of this state will be compared with the unaged state.
- **Conditions:** Both states will undergo isothermal elevated-temperature exposure over 600–1200°C for up to about 100 h. The gas composition will remain unspecified, so conclusions will apply to the oxidation environment used rather than to all oxidizing or reducing gases.
- **Structure through which it acts:** A duplex-to-trilayer scale should form, comprising outer elemental and mixed or spinel oxides, a continuous Cr-rich layer dominated by Cr2O3, and a discontinuous inner Al/Ti-rich region. Cr-depleted precipitates and matrix–precipitate interfaces should delay protective-layer continuity and provide short-circuit oxygen paths.
- **Mechanism:** Initial oxidation should involve loose or discontinuous oxides of multiple constituents. Preferential outward Cr diffusion should then establish dense Cr2O3 that restricts inward oxygen and outward metal-ion transport. Cr should dominate continuous protection because its mobility is more favorable, whereas Al3+ and Ti4+ should diffuse more slowly and their low concentrations should prevent continuous Al/Ti-rich layers. At higher temperature, oxygen should traverse scale defects, microcracks, or grain-boundary channels and thicken the inner oxide.
- **Predicted outcome:** The aged alloy should gain consistently more mass than the as-printed baseline, with the difference increasing with temperature, but without a large overall oxidation penalty. Power-law exponents should be roughly 0.3–0.6 at 600–900°C, with aged material approaching parabolic behavior near 900°C. Both states should show nearly parabolic thickness growth above 900°C and similar apparent activation energies near 150 kJ/mol.
- **Compared with (baseline):** The baseline will be the as-printed single-fcc cellular alloy without L12/L21 precipitates. Its more uniform Cr distribution should permit earlier formation of a protective Cr-rich scale.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Cr2O3-mediated protection should be most effective through about 900°C. Above this threshold, rapid oxygen transport and inner Al/Ti-rich oxidation should become increasingly important. The hypothesis will be limited to isothermal exposures of 600–1200°C and no more than about 100 h.
- **Main uncertainty:** The unspecified gas composition could alter oxide stability and transport, particularly under reducing conditions.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Expose as-printed and 750°C-aged specimens isothermally across 600–1200°C while tracking oxidation kinetics and scale development.
  - *Measurements:* Time-resolved mass gain with power-law exponent and rate-constant fitting; Cross-sectional oxide thickness and Arrhenius activation energy; Cr, Al, Ti, and oxygen distributions across the scale; Identification of Cr-rich, Al/Ti-rich, and mixed oxides
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis will be falsified if aging does not increase mass gain or delay continuous Cr2O3 formation, or if degradation differences occur without corresponding Cr depletion, interfacial oxygen paths, or enhanced inner Al/Ti-rich oxidation.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** The degradation of additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 high-entropy alloy during elevated-temperature exposure to oxidizing or reducing gases is governed by the formation and growth of oxide scales and internal oxidation, leading to catastrophic failure due to the loss of mechanical integrity.
- **Starting material:** Additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 high-entropy alloy with an fcc matrix
- **What is changed or studied:** Exposure to elevated temperatures (800-1000°C) in oxidizing (air) or reducing (argon-hydrogen) gases
- **Conditions:** Temperature range: 800-1000°C, gas environment: air or argon-hydrogen, exposure time: 1-100 hours
- **Structure through which it acts:** Oxide scales and internal oxidation
- **Mechanism:** Thermally activated diffusion of oxygen and metal ions, leading to the formation and growth of oxide scales and internal oxidation
- **Predicted outcome:** Catastrophic failure due to the loss of mechanical integrity, characterized by a significant decrease in tensile strength and ductility
- **Compared with (baseline):** As-fabricated material with no exposure to elevated temperatures or corrosive gases
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Temperature: 800-1000°C, exposure time: 1-100 hours, gas environment: air or argon-hydrogen
- **Main uncertainty:** The exact mechanism of oxide scale formation and growth, as well as the role of internal oxidation, is not well understood and requires further investigation
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Controlled exposure of additively manufactured Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 high-entropy alloy to elevated temperatures (800-1000°C) in oxidizing (air) or reducing (argon-hydrogen) gases, with characterization of microstructure and mechanical properties before and after exposure
  - *Measurements:* Microstructure characterization (SEM, TEM, XRD); Mechanical property testing (tensile testing, hardness testing)
  - *Falsified if:* If no significant degradation or catastrophic failure is observed after exposure to elevated temperatures in oxidizing or reducing gases, the hypothesis is falsified
