## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> Single-phase fcc FeCoNi has high saturation magnetization but a low coercive field, and reported single-phase fcc high-entropy alloys derived from it have coercive fields no greater than 100 Oe in the reported magnetic measurements. What single intervention could overcome this coercivity limitation? Formulate one hypothesis specifying the intervention, its mechanism, the predicted change relative to single-phase fcc FeCoNi or a matched derivative, the relevant compositional, structural, and measurement boundaries, supporting prior evidence, and a controlled experiment with an explicit falsification criterion.

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** Rapid quenching of nominally equiatomic FeCoNiTi will stabilize a ferromagnetic fcc plus hexagonal C14 Laves microstructure whose phase coupling and interfaces will raise the coercive field far beyond the approximately 100 Oe ceiling expected for single-phase fcc FeCoNi-based high-entropy alloys.
- **Starting material:** The target material will be as-cast FeCoNiTi with a nominal Fe:Co:Ni:Ti ratio of 1:1:1:1. A compositionally matched, C14-dominant Fe27Co27Ni17Ti29 alloy will serve as a phase-specific comparator.
- **What is changed or studied:** Rapid solidification on a water-cooled copper hearth after arc melting will be used as the single intervention to retain coexisting fcc and C14 Laves phases rather than a single equilibrium phase.
- **Conditions:** Magnetic behavior will be evaluated through 400 K, with higher-temperature magnetic ordering assessed for the proposed fcc transition near 1100 K. Spin-polarized density-of-states calculations will evaluate the electronic and magnetic states of both phases.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The mediator will be coexistence and interfacial contact between an fcc region of approximately Fe21Co25Ni32Ti22 and a C14 region of approximately Fe27Co27Ni17Ti29, including spin-canted components at or near the dual-phase structure.
- **Mechanism:** Antiferromagnetic coupling between the Fe/Co/Ni and Ti sublattices will reduce saturation magnetization but permit high-temperature ferromagnetic ordering in the fcc phase. The fcc orbital moment, although small in an isolated fcc phase, will contribute to coercivity when coupled to the C14 phase and spin-canted components, enabling domain-wall pinning. Transitions near 1100 K and 170 K will originate from fcc, whereas a transition near 210 K will arise from C14.
- **Predicted outcome:** The dual-phase alloy will reach a coercive field of roughly 650-700 Oe at 400 K, versus no more than about 100 Oe for single-phase fcc FeCoNi or a matched fcc derivative. It will also show three ferromagnetic transitions near 1100, 210, and 170 K.
- **Compared with (baseline):** The baseline will be single-phase fcc Fe-Co-Ni-based alloys, which should have high saturation magnetization, coercivity at or below approximately 100 Oe, and limited orbital-moment-assisted pinning.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The enhancement will require the specified fcc-C14 coexistence, segregation, and antiferromagnetic sublattice coupling. Neither an isolated fcc phase nor a C14-dominant or pure C14 variant should reproduce the combined high coercivity and three-transition response.
- **Main uncertainty:** Rapid solidification may change phase fractions or interfacial connectivity enough to weaken pinning even if both phases remain present.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Compare rapidly quenched dual-phase FeCoNiTi with C14-dominant Fe27Co27Ni17Ti29 and a single-phase fcc FeCoNi-based control under matched magnetic testing.
  - *Measurements:* Phase identity and phase compositions; Coercive field at 400 K; Magnetic transition temperatures; Spin-polarized density of states
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis will be falsified if dual-phase FeCoNiTi does not exceed approximately 100 Oe at 400 K, or if the C14-dominant comparator reproduces the near-1100 K and near-170 K transitions together with comparable coercivity; the comparator should instead show only C14-associated transitions near 250 K and 180 K.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** By subjecting single-phase fcc FeCoNi to a controlled annealing process that induces nanoscale precipitation of an ordered, high-anisotropy phase (e.g., an L1₀-like phase), the magnetic coercivity can be significantly increased while largely preserving the high saturation magnetization.
- **Starting material:** Single-phase fcc FeCoNi (or a closely related high-entropy alloy derivative) with a coercivity below 100 Oe.
- **What is changed or studied:** Thermal treatment (annealing) designed to induce controlled nanoscale precipitation of an ordered phase within the fcc matrix.
- **Conditions:** The annealing process must be performed under conditions (specific temperature and time windows) that promote the formation of nanoscale, ordered precipitates without full decomposition of the fcc phase. The composition remains essentially that of FeCoNi, and the process should be conducted in an inert or reducing atmosphere to prevent oxidation.
- **Structure through which it acts:** Nanoscale precipitates (e.g., L1₀-like ordered regions) embedded within the fcc matrix that create local lattice distortions and act as pinning centers for domain walls.
- **Mechanism:** The formation of ordered precipitates introduces additional magnetocrystalline anisotropy due to the tetragonal distortion inherent to the ordered phase. This enhanced anisotropy increases the energy barrier for domain wall motion, thereby raising the coercive field.
- **Predicted outcome:** An increase in coercivity beyond the &lt;100 Oe level observed in the single-phase fcc alloy, with minimal compromise to the high saturation magnetization characteristic of the base material.
- **Compared with (baseline):** Coercivity and saturation magnetization measurements of the initial single-phase fcc FeCoNi alloy prior to annealing.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis applies as long as the alloy composition is maintained and the annealing treatment is optimized such that only a minority fraction (nanoscale precipitates) forms without triggering a full phase transformation or significantly reducing the ferromagnetic volume fraction.
- **Main uncertainty:** Precise control over precipitate size, distribution, volume fraction, and the corresponding quantitative improvement in coercivity are uncertain and may vary with subtle changes in annealing parameters. Additionally, the exact nature of the ordered phase (and its compatibility with the base fcc matrix) requires further experimental calibration.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare identical samples of single-phase fcc FeCoNi. Subject half of the samples to the designed annealing treatment (followed by rapid cooling or controlled cooling to retain the nanoscale precipitates) while keeping the other half as controls. Use high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) to confirm the presence of nanoscale ordered precipitates in the annealed samples. Measure the magnetic hysteresis loops using vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM) or SQUID magnetometry to determine coercivity and saturation magnetization.
  - *Measurements:* Presence, size, and distribution of precipitates (via HRTEM and XRD); Magnetic hysteresis loops (to extract coercivity and saturation magnetization)
  - *Falsified if:* If the annealed samples do not show a statistically significant increase in coercivity over 100 Oe compared to the control samples, or if the formation of nanoscale ordered precipitates is not observed, then the hypothesis will be falsified.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** Introduction of a secondary phase with high magnetic anisotropy can increase the coercive field of high-entropy alloys derived from single-phase fcc FeCoNi.
- **Starting material:** Single-phase fcc FeCoNi or its high-entropy alloy derivatives
- **What is changed or studied:** Introduction of a secondary phase with high magnetic anisotropy through composition modification or processing techniques
- **Conditions:** The secondary phase should be uniformly distributed and have a size smaller than the magnetic domain size
- **Structure through which it acts:** The secondary phase acts as a pinning site for magnetic domain walls, increasing the energy required for domain wall motion
- **Mechanism:** The introduction of a secondary phase with high magnetic anisotropy increases the magnetic anisotropy of the material, leading to an increase in the coercive field
- **Predicted outcome:** An increase in coercive field greater than 100 Oe compared to single-phase fcc FeCoNi or its high-entropy alloy derivatives
- **Compared with (baseline):** Single-phase fcc FeCoNi or its high-entropy alloy derivatives with coercive fields no greater than 100 Oe
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The secondary phase should not exceed 20% volume fraction to avoid significant reduction in saturation magnetization
- **Main uncertainty:** The optimal composition and processing conditions for introducing the secondary phase are unknown
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare a series of high-entropy alloy samples with varying volume fractions of the secondary phase, measure their coercive fields using a vibrating sample magnetometer, and compare the results to the predicted outcome
  - *Measurements:* Coercive field; Saturation magnetization; Microstructure characterization
  - *Falsified if:* If the coercive field does not increase by at least 20% compared to the baseline for any sample with a secondary phase volume fraction between 5% and 20%, the hypothesis is falsified
