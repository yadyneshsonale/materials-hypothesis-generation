## The research question (same as in Part 1)

> The Co-free FeNiCrMn high-entropy alloy is derived from equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi and is intended to retain a favorable strength–ductility balance in its solidified solid-solution state, but its dynamic behavior across different temperatures and strain rates lacks a theoretical explanation. What single testable mechanistic hypothesis could account for this behavior, and what controlled comparison within that regime would distinguish it? Specify predictions relative to a baseline, applicable boundaries, supporting evidence, and a falsification criterion.

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** The dynamic strength–ductility balance in the Co-free FeNiCrMn high-entropy alloy is primarily governed by a shift in dislocation mechanisms due to a composition‐induced reduction in stacking fault energy. This reduction promotes easier cross‐slip and possibly enhanced twinning at specific temperatures and strain rates, in contrast to the Co-containing baseline.
- **Starting material:** As-solidified, homogeneous single-phase solid-solution state of the Co-free FeNiCrMn alloy.
- **What is changed or studied:** Conduct mechanical tests (e.g., tensile or compression tests) over a controlled range of temperatures and strain rates.
- **Conditions:** Temperature and strain rate regimes spanning from low to high (e.g., sub-ambient up to elevated temperatures and low to high strain rates), where dislocation motion mechanisms are thermally activated.
- **Structure through which it acts:** The stacking fault energy, which modulates the relative ease of dislocation cross-slip versus twinning.
- **Mechanism:** Removing Co lowers the stacking fault energy relative to equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi, thereby modifying the energy barriers for dislocation cross-slip. Under dynamic loading at low temperatures/high strain rates, this yields increased activation of cross-slip and/or twinning, which in turn enhances strain rate sensitivity and affects strength–ductility performance.
- **Predicted outcome:** Relative to the Co-containing baseline, the Co-free alloy is predicted to exhibit a more pronounced increase in flow stress and possibly a shifted balance between work hardening and ductility at low temperatures and/or high strain rates. This change should be measurable as a lower activation volume and altered dislocation substructure (e.g., more twins or cross-slip features) in microstructural analysis.
- **Compared with (baseline):** The equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy with Co present.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis is expected to hold within the temperature and strain rate regimes where dislocation mechanisms are sensitive to stacking fault energy changes. Outside these regimes (e.g., very high temperatures where diffusion dominates), the mechanism may not be the primary factor.
- **Main uncertainty:** There is uncertainty as to the quantitative influence of stacking fault energy changes versus potential contributions from short-range ordering or other atomic-scale effects that may also evolve with temperature and strain rate.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare samples of both the Co-free FeNiCrMn alloy and the Co-containing CoCrFeMnNi alloy. Subject both sets to monotonic tensile or compression tests at a series of temperatures (e.g., from sub-ambient to moderate elevated temperatures) and strain rates (from quasi-static to high strain rate). Characterize the mechanical response (flow stress, strain rate sensitivity, activation volume) and examine the dislocation structures (via TEM) post-deformation to identify signs of cross-slip or twinning.
  - *Measurements:* Flow stress versus strain curves at each temperature and strain rate; Activation volume calculations from strain rate sensitivity data; Microstructural analysis for twin formation and dislocation cross-slip features; Stacking fault energy estimates (if feasible) via indirect or modeling methods
  - *Falsified if:* If the Co-free alloy does not exhibit significantly different strain rate sensitivity, activation volume, or dislocation substructure (e.g., no enhanced cross-slip or twinning) compared to the baseline Co-containing alloy under the specified conditions, then the hypothesis that the dynamic behavior is primarily driven by a stacking fault energy–mediated shift in dislocation mechanisms will be falsified.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** The dynamic behavior of the Co-free FeNiCrMn high-entropy alloy across different temperatures and strain rates can be explained by a mechanism involving the interaction between dislocation glide and twinning, which affects the alloy's strength and ductility.
- **Starting material:** Solidified solid-solution state
- **What is changed or studied:** Variation of temperature and strain rate
- **Conditions:** Different temperatures and strain rates
- **Structure through which it acts:** Dislocation glide and twinning
- **Mechanism:** Interaction between dislocation glide and twinning
- **Predicted outcome:** The alloy's strength and ductility will vary with temperature and strain rate, with a potential trade-off between the two
- **Compared with (baseline):** The behavior of the equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Temperature range: -200°C to 800°C, strain rate range: 10^-4 s^-1 to 10^3 s^-1
- **Main uncertainty:** The exact nature of the interaction between dislocation glide and twinning, and the resulting effects on the alloy's behavior
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Tensile testing at various temperatures and strain rates, with microstructural analysis to observe dislocation glide and twinning
  - *Measurements:* Stress-strain curves; Microstructural images
  - *Falsified if:* If the observed behavior does not match the predicted trade-off between strength and ductility, or if the microstructural analysis does not show evidence of dislocation glide and twinning, the hypothesis is falsified

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** In single-crystal equiatomic FCC FeNiCrMn, yielding under high-rate tension will be initiated by dislocation nucleation at vacancy clusters or locally disordered atoms rather than by a bulk phase transformation. Shockley partial slip and associated stacking faults will therefore explain the alloy’s temperature softening, orientation anisotropy, and rate-dependent post-yield flow while leaving the yield stress nearly rate-insensitive.
- **Starting material:** The starting material will be a bulk, periodic, equiatomic FeNiCrMn high-entropy-alloy single crystal with an initially FCC solid-solution structure.
- **What is changed or studied:** Molecular-dynamics simulations will apply uniaxial tensile loading along &lt;110&gt;, &lt;1-12&gt;, and &lt;-111&gt; while independently varying temperature and strain rate.
- **Conditions:** Loading will be evaluated at approximately 10, 300, and 600 K and strain rates of 10^8, 10^9, and 10^10 s^-1. The model will be thermally prepared near 1000 K and cooled to the reference state before loading.
- **Structure through which it acts:** Plasticity will be mediated primarily by 1/6&lt;112&gt; Shockley partial dislocations nucleating at vacancy-rich or disordered regions. Their motion will generate intrinsic and extrinsic stacking faults and local HCP-like atomic environments without producing a bulk FCC-to-BCC transformation.
- **Mechanism:** Yielding will coincide with abrupt dislocation nucleation and a sudden stress drop. Increasing temperature will amplify thermal vibrations, lower the critical resolved shear stress for nucleation, and reduce yield strength. Increasing strain rate will limit dislocation annihilation, raising dislocation density and post-yield flow stress, but it will not substantially shift the nucleation-controlled yield strength within the simulated rate window.
- **Predicted outcome:** Relative to the 300 K, 10^9 s^-1 baseline, yield strength will decrease at 600 K and increase at 10 K. The &lt;-111&gt; loading direction will have the highest yield stress. Changing strain rate by two orders of magnitude will produce little change in yield strength but will increase dislocation density and flow stress at 10^10 s^-1. Twinning and bulk BCC formation will remain absent.
- **Compared with (baseline):** The reference comparison will be equiatomic FeNiCrMn loaded at 300 K and 10^9 s^-1, with crystallographic orientation held fixed for temperature and rate comparisons.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis will apply to periodic single crystals, temperatures of about 10–600 K, and strain rates of 10^8–10^10 s^-1, provided deformation remains dominated by partial-dislocation slip rather than twinning or bulk transformation.
- **Main uncertainty:** The apparent rate insensitivity of yield may depend on the modeled vacancy population and might not extend beyond the simulated rate window or to polycrystals.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* At fixed orientation and 300 K, compare tension at 10^8, 10^9, and 10^10 s^-1; separately compare 10, 300, and 600 K at 10^9 s^-1.
  - *Measurements:* Stress–strain curves and yield-stress changes; DXA dislocation types and densities; CNA fractions of FCC, HCP-like, and BCC environments
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis will be rejected if yielding occurs without an abrupt partial-dislocation nucleation signature, if a sustained bulk BCC phase or twinning develops, or if yield stress changes strongly and systematically with strain rate while dislocation-density kinetics do not explain the flow-stress trend.
