## The research question

> For AlCrFeNiSi powder feedstock intended for additively manufactured miniature components, what single intervention could overcome the residual thermal stresses, degraded mechanical properties, and processing complexity associated with mainstream metallic additive-manufacturing regimes? Formulate one hypothesis that specifies the intervention and mechanism, predicts outcomes relative to components made under conventional high-thermal-load additive-manufacturing conditions, states the applicable processing window, geometry, and test conditions, identifies supporting prior evidence, and describes a falsification experiment.

The three hypotheses below were written for this question. Read all three, then rate each one on its own merits.

---

## Hypothesis A

- **Main hypothesis:** The application of a controlled, low-thermal-load selective laser sintering (SLS) technique can mitigate residual thermal stresses and improve mechanical properties in AlCrFeNiSi powder feedstock for additively manufactured miniature components.
- **Starting material:** AlCrFeNiSi powder feedstock
- **What is changed or studied:** Controlled, low-thermal-load selective laser sintering (SLS) technique
- **Conditions:** Low thermal load, optimized laser scanning strategy, and controlled atmosphere
- **Structure through which it acts:** Microstructure and porosity
- **Mechanism:** Reduced thermal gradients and stresses, improved densification, and minimized oxidation
- **Predicted outcome:** Improved mechanical properties (e.g., strength, ductility) and reduced residual stresses compared to conventional high-thermal-load additive manufacturing
- **Compared with (baseline):** Components made under conventional high-thermal-load additive-manufacturing conditions
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** Miniature components with complex geometries, thickness &lt; 1 mm
- **Main uncertainty:** Insufficient data on the specific effects of low-thermal-load SLS on AlCrFeNiSi powder feedstock
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Comparative study of miniature components made using low-thermal-load SLS and conventional high-thermal-load additive manufacturing
  - *Measurements:* Residual stress analysis; Mechanical property testing (tensile strength, ductility); Microstructural analysis (SEM, EBSD)
  - *Falsified if:* If the measured residual stresses and mechanical properties of components made using low-thermal-load SLS are not significantly improved compared to those made using conventional high-thermal-load additive manufacturing, the hypothesis is falsified.

## Hypothesis B

- **Main hypothesis:** Oxygen-deficient debinding of DLP-printed AlCrFeNiSi powder–resin green bodies would provide a lower-thermal-load route to miniature components by suppressing debinding cracks and enabling near-complete consolidation and approximately 5 MPa compressive strength at about 1050 °C rather than 1100 °C.
- **Starting material:** Homogeneous gas-atomized AlCrFeNiSi high-entropy-alloy powder would be dispersed in an acrylate-based photocurable resin and DLP-printed as layered miniature green bodies.
- **What is changed or studied:** The single intervention would be replacement of oxygen-rich ambient-air debinding with oxygen-deficient debinding maintained by continuous evacuation. Debinding would include stages near 300 and 450 °C and proceed to about 500 °C, followed by solid-state sintering.
- **Conditions:** Oxygen-deficient and oxygen-rich debinding would be compared for otherwise equivalent DLP green bodies subsequently sintered between 800 and 1100 °C. Mechanical performance would be evaluated by compression testing, with morphology and chemistry assessed on exterior and polished cross-sectional surfaces.
- **Structure through which it acts:** Oxygen-deficient debinding would retain more carbon in former resin regions, potentially about 34 at.% rather than 22 at.%, while limiting oxygen to roughly 4 at.% rather than 9 at.%. This state would reduce pore volume and cracking, accelerate removal of layered and particle-boundary morphology, and strengthen interparticle bonding.
- **Mechanism:** Restricted oxygen would limit rapid conversion of resin and powder constituents into escaping CO and CO2, thereby reducing pressure-driven surface cracking. Lower oxide coverage would offer less resistance to interparticle mass transport, while retained carbon and reduced porosity would facilitate diffusion, interfacial bonding, and load transfer. Thermally activated elemental interdiffusion would then progressively consolidate the particles.
- **Predicted outcome:** Oxygen-deficient specimens would lose their layered morphology and approach saturated consolidation near 1050 °C, approximately 50 °C below the oxygen-rich baseline, while reaching about 5 MPa compressive strength and showing fewer surface cracks. At 800 °C, strength could be roughly 2–3 MPa versus about 0.5 MPa for oxygen-rich specimens.
- **Compared with (baseline):** Ambient-air-debound DLP specimens would require approximately 1100 °C for full consolidation and about 5 MPa compressive strength, with greater debinding-induced cracking, porosity, and persistent particle or layer boundaries at lower temperatures.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The hypothesis would apply to the specified AlCrFeNiSi/acrylate DLP system, staged debinding route, miniature layered geometry, and 800–1100 °C sintering window. Below about 1000 °C, diffusion would remain limited. Near 1100 °C, oxygen-deficient material could form stripe-like precipitates through localized softening or melting of fine, oxide-unprotected regions; substantially lower temperatures could yield mechanically unstable bodies.
- **Main uncertainty:** Residual carbon could either assist consolidation or alter local phase stability, making the balance between improved bonding and high-temperature precipitate formation uncertain.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare otherwise equivalent DLP AlCrFeNiSi green bodies, debind them under oxygen-rich air or continuous evacuation, and sinter matched groups from 800 to 1100 °C before compression testing.
  - *Measurements:* SEM assessment of cracks, porosity, particle interfaces, layered morphology, and consolidation; EDS mapping of carbon, oxygen, aluminum, nickel, and interfacial redistribution; Compressive strength as a function of debinding atmosphere and sintering temperature
  - *Falsified if:* The hypothesis would be falsified if oxygen-deficient specimens sintered near 1050 °C did not show reduced cracking and earlier morphological consolidation, or if their compressive strength remained materially below the approximately 5 MPa attained by oxygen-rich specimens near 1100 °C.

## Hypothesis C

- **Main hypothesis:** Applying an ultrafast pulsed laser-based additive manufacturing process to AlCrFeNiSi feedstock will reduce residual thermal stresses and improve mechanical properties by limiting overall thermal exposure during fabrication.
- **Starting material:** AlCrFeNiSi powder feedstock with as-atomized microstructure prepared for additive manufacturing.
- **What is changed or studied:** Replace conventional high-thermal-load processing with an ultrafast pulsed laser melting regime (e.g., femtosecond laser pulses) that delivers energy in extremely short bursts to minimize melt-pool lifetime and overall heat accumulation.
- **Conditions:** Fabricate miniature components (characteristic dimensions &lt;5 mm) in an inert atmosphere; use pulse durations in the femtosecond range with fluences tuned to create melt pools of ~10–50 µm, ensuring rapid solidification and minimal heat diffusion.
- **Structure through which it acts:** A refined, near-equilibrium microstructure featuring ultrafine grains and suppressed phase growth that minimizes residual thermal stresses.
- **Mechanism:** The ultrafast pulsed laser intervention reduces cumulative thermal input, thereby limiting thermal gradients and cooling-induced residual stresses. Rapid solidification favors homogeneous microstructure and improved mechanical integrity compared to the coarser, stress-laden structure from conventional high-thermal-load processes.
- **Predicted outcome:** Components fabricated using the ultrafast pulsed laser process are expected to exhibit significantly lower residual thermal stresses and enhanced mechanical properties (e.g., higher tensile strength and hardness) relative to those made by conventional processing.
- **Compared with (baseline):** Conventional high-thermal-load additive manufacturing, where prolonged exposure to heat creates larger melt pools, higher residual stresses, and degraded mechanical properties.
- **Where it should hold (boundary):** The benefits may be limited to miniature geometries (&lt;5 mm) and to feedstocks with similar compositions; scale-up to larger components or different alloy systems may not reproduce these effects.
- **Main uncertainty:** While the qualitative improvements are supported by the notion of reduced thermal exposure, the quantitative extent of stress reduction and mechanical enhancement remains uncertain without further experimental verification.
- **Test that would falsify it:**
  - *Design:* Prepare two sets of miniature components from the same batch of AlCrFeNiSi powder. Process one set using the ultrafast pulsed laser intervention (femtosecond pulsing under specified fluence and pulse duration conditions) and the other set using conventional high-thermal-load additive manufacturing. Ensure that all other parameters (atmosphere, substrate, and post-processing) are held constant. Characterize the components for residual thermal stresses, microstructural features, and mechanical properties.
  - *Measurements:* Residual stress via X-ray diffraction analysis; Microhardness testing; Tensile strength measurements; Microstructural analysis using SEM/TEM to assess grain size and phase distribution
  - *Falsified if:* If the ultrafast pulsed laser processed components do not exhibit a statistically significant reduction (e.g., at least 25% lower residual stresses) and meaningful improvement in mechanical properties compared to the conventional process set, then the hypothesis will be falsified.
